Case study 02 / Platform strategy

One Search, Every Matter

Building a shared, permission-aware search platform across legal workflows.

Platform strategyCross-source qualityFailure-driven iteration
Independent, hypothetical case study. Every scenario, dataset, result, timeline, and team constraint is assumed or simulated. This is not a Clio project and does not represent access to its customers, telemetry, architecture, staff, or plans. It demonstrates decisions I would test, not work shipped or outcomes achieved.
Inside this studyThe decisionEvidence workflowPlatform contractsQuality standardExperiment explorerThe iterationRoadmap & leadership
01 / decision

Prove one complete task before expanding.

A lawyer asks: “Where did the client first approve the revised payment schedule?” The fictional evidence spans an intake note, an email, and a revised agreement. A document-only index can return a relevant contract while missing the original approval entirely.

My proposed decision

Start with one permission-safe, matter-scoped task across documents and a single communications source. Expand only after coverage, freshness, relevance, and trust pass explicit gates.

The platform foundation

Correctness comes from contracts.

Identity, matter links, permission policies, lifecycle events, and canonical URLs require authoritative source data.

The selective AI layer

Meaning can improve retrieval.

Test semantic matching, passage discovery, and reranking only among eligible, authorized candidates.

03 / contracts

Specify the foundation before promising universal search.

The conceptual service normalizes source records, maintains permission-aware full-text and vector indexes, and exposes retrieval profiles through a shared API. Human search and downstream AI consumers need separate quality evaluation.

ContractRequired evidence
Identity & provenanceSource product, immutable record ID, canonical link, passage offsets
Matter contextExplicit authoritative relationships; unknown stays unknown
Content & lifecycleTitle/body, known status, source and index timestamps, replay-safe update/delete events
Access & processingTenant boundary, ACL reference, purpose-specific processing policy

Clear ownership boundaries

The platform owns ingestion contracts, index health, authorization plumbing, retrieval tooling, and APIs. Consumer teams own task-specific presentation, filters, and rollout. Source teams own the truth of original records and events.

Search indexing, model inference, and model training are separate processing purposes. Permission checks must cover candidates, snippets, caches, logging, AI contexts, and delivered results.

04 / evaluation

Measure the whole task, including missing sources.

An assumed benchmark of 120 synthetic matter-level tasks labels every required record, provenance, matter relationship, and authorization for each test persona. Use adjudicated judgments and a held-out task set; report results by source and consumer profile.

Decision on the simulated v1

Stop rollout. One critical authorization failure is sufficient, even though required-source coverage rises from 62% to 91%.

The imagined failure review reveals incorrect matter joins, incomparable per-source ranking scores, stale access policies, and delayed lifecycle events. A stronger embedding model cannot repair those contracts.

06 / iteration

Fix correctness. Then selectively apply AI.

  1. Use authoritative relationships.

    Require source IDs and explicit matter links; never guess from matching contact names or semantic similarity.

  2. Fail closed when permissions are uncertain.

    Enforce user-scoped access at query and result time, and invalidate affected caches on revocation.

  3. Make updates and deletions dependable.

    Use replay-safe events, deletion tombstones, freshness alerts, and reconciliation against source-of-truth counts.

  4. Calibrate retrieval across sources.

    Evaluate fusion instead of merging raw scores. Test semantic passage retrieval only after coverage and authorization are dependable.

Conditional release decision

Consider a tightly scoped pilot after independent security review, source reconciliation, held-out evaluation, and live-user task observation.

Zero failed synthetic ACL tests does not certify security. An AI assistant consuming the platform needs its own evaluation of required-evidence coverage, citation support, and unsupported assertions.

07 / execution

Expand when the first slice earns it.

Assumed setting: a shared search squad with one PM, six engineers, two ML engineers, part-time source owners, and security partners over two quarters.

Quarter 1 / Prove one end-to-end job

Documents + one communications source.

Agree on contracts, test access and deletion flows, instrument coverage, and benchmark evidence completeness. Resolve failures before a pilot.

Gate: the first slice meets trust, freshness, relevance, and task-success standards.
Quarter 2 / Expand conditionally

Add one source or consumer profile.

If the foundation passes, extend it carefully. If an event stream or matter mapping is unreliable, fund that repair before another integration.

Pivot: if >50% of priority misses come from within-source ranking, prioritize relevance instead.

How I would lead the decision

Own user-job prioritization, trade-offs, explicit gates, and sequencing. Keep a shared decision log with the task, suspected cause, alternative, evidence required, owner, gate, and rollback trigger. Use weekly dependency reviews and end-to-end demos to expose risks early.

Open questions: frequency and value of cross-source tasks, reliability of matter links, source eligibility, consumer-specific freshness budgets, and the incremental value of semantic retrieval after deterministic correctness is secured.
