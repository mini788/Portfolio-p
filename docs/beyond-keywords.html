Case study 01 / AI search & experimentation

Making legal document search measurably better.

01 / decision

Start with the job. Then choose the technology.

A legal professional needs the correct, authorized, up-to-date source—even when they remember its meaning instead of its title. Semantic retrieval can connect paraphrases. It cannot find an absent email, repair permissions, or establish which contract is signed.

My proposed decision

Improve lexical search first. Add hybrid retrieval selectively where meaning mismatch is the demonstrated bottleneck.

Known document

“Acme NDA 2025”

Prioritize exact filenames, matter IDs, and citations. A thematically similar agreement is the wrong result.

Remembered meaning

“Termination without a penalty”

Test semantic candidates against a clause that says “may withdraw without an early exit fee.” Retrieve the original passage.

03 / evaluation

Define “good” before the experiment.

The north-star outcome would be Verified Search Task Success: can the user correctly identify and open the requested authorized source with the required matter and version evidence? Click-through rate alone cannot answer that question.

Assumed benchmark

3,000 fictional records · 240 queries

60 known-item, 70 semantic, 45 contextual, 35 metadata, and 30 difficult queries. This is an evaluation design, not a production traffic mix.

Evaluation discipline

Adjudicated labels. A protected holdout.

Two hypothetical reviewers judge relevance from 0–3. Tune on a development set; deduplicate close paraphrases across splits and report uncertainty.

Assumed release gates

Actual gates require baseline measurement, user tolerance testing, and cost analysis. Offline synthetic labels cannot establish real-user task success.

Decision on the simulated v1

Do not ship. A roughly 22% semantic relevance gain does not compensate for a seven-point exact-item regression, or for exceeding latency and cost budgets.

In the imagined error review, 12 of 60 known-item queries lose their correct first result. That is a query-level failure pattern, distinct from the aggregate Success@1 change. I would test three hypotheses: vector scores overpower identifiers, short queries take the wrong route, and reranking adds unnecessary expense.

05 / iteration

Improve by narrowing where AI is used.

  1. Route identifiers to lexical search.

    Use deterministic detection for high-confidence matter IDs, filenames, and citations. Cap semantic influence when an exact match exists.

  2. Preserve structured constraints.

    Signed status, dates, and authorization must remain hard filters. An unknown status stays unknown.

  3. Spend reranking where it adds value.

    Use it for complex exploratory queries only when an ablation supports the added latency and cost. Keep caches within the correct authorization context.

  4. Help people verify the source.

    Display grounded matched passages, canonical links, matter context, and authoritative version metadata. No generative answer cards in the first release.

The simulated v2 gives up some semantic gain to restore exact precision and meet the assumed operational gates. That is a trade-off worth investigating, not a claim of success.

Conditional recommendation

Investigate a narrow rollout only after a new holdout, access-control checks, shadow testing, and real-task validation.

06 / execution

A roadmap with decisions built in.

Assumed setting: one PM, one designer, five search engineers, two ML engineers, and security support over 12 weeks. This is a planning exercise, not a company staffing claim.

Weeks 1–3

Map failures and approve the evaluation.

Define synthetic labels, instrument traces, and test the actual source of missed searches.

Gate: is meaning mismatch a material, addressable failure?
Weeks 4–6

Compare retrieval options and ablations.

Evaluate lexical improvements and hybrid v1 by query segment.

Gate: segment uplift without critical regression?
Weeks 7–9

Iterate routing, ranking, and verification UX.

Retest on a protected holdout and review worst-query failures weekly.

Gate: quality and operational thresholds passed?
Weeks 10–12

Shadow test, observe tasks, decide.

Use a limited randomized rollout only with safe exposure, sufficient power, and prespecified rollback conditions.

Gate: evidence to expand, or a reason to roll back?

What I would own

User-value definition, prioritization, acceptance thresholds, and documented trade-offs. Search and ML engineers own implementation quality; design tests verification usability; security and privacy validate data handling. Launch decisions are joint and evidence-gated.

Still unresolved: real-user impact, uncertainty in the small benchmark, critical-citation regressions, deleted-file behavior, and sufficient statistical power to rule out known-item harm.
